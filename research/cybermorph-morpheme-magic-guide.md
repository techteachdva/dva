# CyberMorph + Morpheme Magic: a teacher's guide

*For Mr. Phil, 19 Oct 2026. How to use CyberMorph as supplemental practice alongside Morpheme Magic (3rd ed.), not as a replacement.*

## The short answer

- **Morpheme Magic teaches. CyberMorph practices.** Students meet a morpheme first in your Monday Hear–Say / Read–Write lesson. The game comes after that, never before.
- **Short and spaced:** 10–15 minutes, two or three times a week, inside the lesson's "Activities" slot (Tuesday to Thursday). Never one long session.
- **The game's data feeds your warm-up.** Morpheme Magic opens Tuesday to Friday with "a morphological problem to solve: current morpheme or review." The game's misses tell you which problem to pose.
- **One gap to fix:** the game's story rooms are ordered by origin (Anglo-Saxon, Latin, Greek). Morpheme Magic is ordered by type (suffixes, then prefixes, then roots, then Greek). Week to week they won't line up unless the game can follow your lesson number. That is the first thing I'd build (see the last section).

## What Morpheme Magic asks for, and where the game fits

From the publisher's sample (overview, lesson details, scope and sequence, sample schedule):

| Morpheme Magic part | Who does it | Game's role |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction: Hear–Say, Read–Write | You, explicitly | None. Don't replace this. |
| Key words, key sentence, "scoop" the morphemes | You | None. The game's DEBUG cut is the same move, so name it: "scoop it in the game the way we scoop on the board." |
| Connect passage, focus words | You | None. |
| Morpheme Lexicon entry | Students, on paper | The game's Lexicon tree is a bonus, not the record. Keep the paper lexicon. |
| Activities (several days) | You choose | **This is the game's slot.** |
| Daily "morphological problem to solve" | You | **Pick it from the game's misses** (see "Data into instruction"). |
| Write | Students | None. Writing stays in class. |
| End-of-lesson assessment (Friday) | Students | The game's numbers are a check against it, not a replacement. |

The research agrees with this split:

- Morphology instruction works best as part of a fuller literacy program. In Goodwin and Ahn's 2010 meta-analysis, morphology inside comprehensive instruction had a significant effect (d = 0.36). Morphology taught in isolation did not.
- Games help most as a supplement over several sessions. Wouters et al. (2013) found serious games beat conventional instruction on learning (d = 0.29). The effect was larger when games were combined with other instruction, played across multiple sessions, and played in groups. Games were *not* more motivating on average, so the fun has to come from the classroom too.
- Shorter, spaced practice beats longer, massed practice for vocabulary retention. That is why I suggest 10–15 minutes, two or three times a week.

## The weekly rhythm

One Morpheme Magic lesson per week, following its sample schedule:

| Day | Morpheme Magic | CyberMorph |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Introduce and teach the lesson morpheme. Key words, focus words, lexicon entry, Morpheme Board. | **No game.** Explicit teaching first. |
| **Tue** | Warm-up problem (current morpheme). Review focus words. | 10–15 min: this week's morpheme, with last weeks' mixed in. |
| **Wed** | Warm-up from **Tuesday's game misses**. Activities. | 10–15 min, or skip if Tuesday was strong. |
| **Thu** | Warm-up. Activities. Write with focus words. | 10–15 min **review mix**: older morphemes only (spaced retrieval). |
| **Fri** | Lesson assessment. Write in response to reading. | Optional, for early finishers. Compare game numbers with the assessment. |

Rules of thumb:

- **Accuracy before speed.** Overclock Mode (the 2-minute timed mode) only on morphemes at least two weeks old. A timer on a brand-new morpheme rewards guessing.
- **Pairs on Tuesdays** (one drives, one explains the cut out loud). Wouters found group play adds to the effect, and talking through a cut is the "scoop" routine said aloud.
- **End every session the same way:** "Name one morpheme you DEBUGged and its meaning." It takes 30 seconds and doubles as an exit ticket.

## Sequencing the year

Morpheme Magic offers three ways to order the 52 lessons. Pick one, then match the game to it.

1. **In order** (all 13 suffixes, then 19 prefixes, then 18 roots, then 2 Greek). That is more than a school year at one lesson a week.
2. **Interleaved:** two or three prefixes, two or three suffixes, two or three roots, repeat. **This is the one I'd pick for grades 6–8.** It mixes types the way the game's waves do, and middle schoolers already know the inflectional suffixes (-s, -ed, -ing). Morpheme Magic's own notes call those review for older students, so Suffix 1–4 can be quick.
3. **Matched to your reading or content lessons.** It works with any of the above.

What this means in practice:

- **Anglo-Saxon Room** = most of the suffix unit, plus five prefixes (un-, over-, mis-, fore-, mid-). Open it once the suffixes are done. Treat those five prefixes as a preview with Old Wynn's lesson cards.
- **Latin Room** = the rest of the prefixes and all 18 roots, spread over six mentor lessons. It is long. Students reach roots before you have taught them unless the game can follow your lesson (below).
- **Greek Room, the Rot finale and the Lingo Room** fit the end of the year: Greek 1–2, then a celebration. The Lingo Room is a good reward day.

## Limits and constraints

**Time**
- 10–15 minutes a session, two or three sessions a week. Set a visible class timer. The game saves after every task, so stopping mid-room loses nothing.
- No session longer than 20 minutes. Past that, it's entertainment, not spaced practice.

**Content**
- Students only practise what you've already taught. Until the game can follow your lesson number, use these levers:
  - Gate the story: "Today we play only up to Old Wynn's third lesson."
  - Share focused links: add `?set=` to the game address with this week's morphemes, e.g. `/cybermorph/?set=de,trans`. Hub review Rotlings and Overclock Mode then stick to those morphemes (Overclock needs at least four matching words, and unlocks after the Anglo-Saxon Room).
- Overclock only for morphemes at least two weeks old. Speed is for fluency, after accuracy.

**Behaviour**
- Pairs on new material, solo on review.
- The Lingo Room, the d20 and Made-up Words are rewards. Open them on a celebration day, not during practice.
- Saves live in each browser. Keep one Chromebook per student, or students lose progress.

**Equity and access**
- Settings has text size, speech speed and reduced motion. Turn speech on for students who need to hear the morphemes. That matches Morpheme Magic's Hear–Say step.
- A wrong answer in the game only shoves you back a step. Keep it low-stakes in class too: game numbers inform your teaching. They are not a grade.

## Turning game data into instruction

What the game records today, per student, in the browser:

- every morpheme they've met, with a mastery level (0–4 stars), first-try hits / tries, and whether it is fading (due for review);
- words compiled, Rotlings debugged, first-try rate;
- a **Progress report** (pause menu) that prints on one page, morphemes grouped by mastery, with "Needs review" boxed first;
- per class session: tries, first tries and mistakes by kind for every morpheme, packed into the exit-ticket code (see below).

How to use it each week (the thresholds are my suggestions; adjust to your class):

| What you see | What it likely means | What to do in class |
|---|---|---|
| This week's morpheme under about 70% first try for many students | Meaning not secure yet | Reteach in Wednesday's warm-up: key word, scoop, two focus words. |
| Real words judged as "Rot fakes," or fakes accepted as real | Spelling joints aren't automatic: drop-e, double, y→i, assimilation | Mini-lesson on Morpheme Magic's "double it, change it, drop it" rules, using the exact words they missed. |
| Wrong spelling picked from a family (tain for ten, ible for able, ance for ence) | They know the meaning, but not which form a word takes | Sort activity: word cards into the family's columns (the wall card lists them). |
| An older morpheme marked "needs review" for several students | Forgetting, which is normal | Put it in Thursday's review mix and in Friday's "Write" word bank. |
| Strong game numbers, weak Friday assessment | Recognition without production | More Write tasks. The game mostly tests recognising parts, not using words. |

## Class sessions, exit tickets and the class report (built)

**Exit Ticket mode (the default class link).** Students click their name and go straight into a focused session on only the lessons you ticked: no story, no Hub. They see today's morphemes on a lesson card, then alternate COMPILE rounds ("build the word that means…") and DEBUG rounds (split it, then real word or Rot?) built from those morphemes, until your timer ends. Then the exit ticket. Only the ticked morphemes are recorded, so the ticket and your class report stay on the lesson. If a student closes a round, the game asks whether to finish early. Words use only ticked morphemes plus everyday words and whatever that student has already learned; when that's too few (one lesson ticked, a brand-new student), it adds words with the fewest unfamiliar parts. Tick earlier lessons too for more variety. Choose "Full game" on the teacher page for the story with a timer instead.

**Before the first class (once):**
1. Make a Google Form with one Short answer question, "Paste your CyberMorph code".
2. Form menu ⋮ → **Get pre-filled link**, type `CODE` in the answer, copy the link.
3. Open the class report page (`/cybermorph/teacher.html`), paste the form link under "Make a class link", pick the session length, and tick this week's focus lessons. Copy the class link and post it in Google Classroom.

**What students see:**
- A clock under the objective card. One minute before the end: "Finish the task you are on." At zero, the game waits for the current task to finish, then shows the **exit ticket**:
  - what they practised, by Morpheme Magic lesson (first-try score for each morpheme);
  - **Say it**: a partner prompt on one morpheme (with a Check button);
  - **Look again next time**: the morphemes they made mistakes on;
  - their **report code**, with COPY CODE and OPEN THE FORM (the code is already filled in, so they just press Submit).
- After a timed session, that browser shows "Session complete" for 45 minutes (they can reopen their exit ticket). It's a soft lock: opening the game without your link skips it.
- Without a timer, students can end a session any time from the pause menu (End session · exit ticket).

**What you see (teacher.html):** paste the code column from the response sheet. You get:
- class totals for the date range (last 7 days by default);
- **warm-up picks**: the lessons under 80% first try, with the main mistake behind each and a move for your daily "morphological problem to solve";
- **students × lessons**: first-try % per student per Morpheme Magic lesson, colour coded, with tries and mistakes on hover;
- **lessons and mistakes**: every lesson's tries, first-try %, mistakes by kind, and how many students have it fading.

Mistakes are sorted by kind, and each kind points to a different move:

| Kind | Means | Move |
|---|---|---|
| seam | cut the word in the wrong place | Scoop three words together on the board. |
| joint | missed a spelling change (drop-e, double, y→i, assimilation) | Double it, change it, drop it, with their words. |
| order | accepted parts in the wrong order | Prefix · base · suffix card sort. |
| link | missed a Greek linking o | Sort Greek words with and without the o. |
| real | called a real word fake (usually one with a spelling change) | Prove real words with a word sum. |
| family | right meaning, wrong spelling (tain/ten, able/ible, ance/ence) | Family sort from the wall card. |
| meaning | picked a part with the wrong meaning | Key word, key sentence, two focus words. |

Codes carry the student's name as typed in the game, the date, minutes, words built and Rotlings debugged, this session's tries and mistakes by lesson, and all-time mastery by lesson. Nothing is uploaded by the game or the report page; the only place codes live is your own form.

## What's next

- **Morpheme Magic mode**: pick the lesson you're on, and the story and the waves only use what you've taught, weighted to this week with spaced review. That fixes the ordering gap above.
- **This week's word list**: type your wall card's focus words and the game uses them as Rotlings.
