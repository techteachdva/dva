TECH ESCAPE 2.0

The lab is closed. Something else is logged in.

It is 9:47 PM. You stayed late to finish your project and the Tech Lab doors just locked behind you. The lights are dead. Four Chromebooks still glow in the dark — and something is skittering between the tables.

Clear the security check on all 4 Chromebooks to earn 4 pieces of an encrypted code. Crack each piece in the decryption scramble, then reach the 3D printer, print the key, and get out before they find you.

Desks are solid. Crouch with C to crawl under one — the glowing tape means you can hide there. Nothing can reach you underneath.

Your flashlight is a weapon — but only against viruses. Hold the beam on a floating virus and it glitches out and teleports away. It comes back. Burning one drains your battery fast, and the light attracts the mice.

Hot cheetos are a decision. Eat a bag with R to get health back, or throw with E — mice crowd the bag and pop one by one after they eat enough. Each bag can take out three mice.

An anti-virus disc deletes a virus for good. They are rare. Aim at a virus and press E; if you miss, the disc clatters to the floor and you can pick it back up.

WHAT WILL I LEARN?

Every Chromebook in the lab quizzes you on real Middle School Technology content. Get it right and you earn a piece of the code. Get it wrong and the noise brings something running.

The Design Process

DEFINE the problem → PREPARE a plan and research → TRY a prototype → REFLECT on the results and iterate. Terminal 1 drills this cycle hard, including what belongs in each phase and what does not.

Code.org Middle School strands

  • Computing Systems — hardware, software, input/output, troubleshooting.
  • Networks & the Internet — packets, protocols, addressing, encryption.
  • Data & Analysis — collection, bias, visualization, models.
  • Algorithms & Programming — loops, conditionals, variables, debugging.
  • Impacts of Computing — digital citizenship, safety, ethics, AI.

ISTE Standards for Students (2025)

  • Empowered Learner — set goals, use feedback, choose the right tool.
  • Digital Citizen — manage your footprint, credit sources, stay safe.
  • Knowledge Constructor — evaluate sources, build meaning from evidence.
  • Innovative Designer — use a design process, tolerate ambiguity, iterate.
  • Computational Thinker — decompose, find patterns, test with data.
  • Creative Communicator — pick formats and remix responsibly.
  • Global Collaborator — work with others toward a shared goal.
