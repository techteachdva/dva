In Feleco, we use it to model natural density-dependent effects — like overcrowding mortality — so
     that changes happen gradually rather than with sudden hard thresholds.

    The mathematical form is:

    f(x) = L / (1 + e^(-k(x - x₀)))

    Where:
    - e is Euler's constant (~2.71828)
    - L is the maximum value (e.g., maximum mortality rate)
    - x₀ is the midpoint where the curve is at 50%
    - k controls steepness (how sharply the transition happens)

    In ecology, this is the natural shape of population growth limits. A real population doesn't
    instantly crash the moment it hits a number — instead, the death rate from starvation and crowding
    rises along this smooth S-curve as resources get scarcer. This is exactly what we replaced the old
    hard-coded culling thresholds with in _apply_overpopulation().

    Visually, it looks like a stretched "S" lying on its side: flat near zero, then steeply rising near
     the carrying capacity, then flattening out again at the maximum mortality rate.
